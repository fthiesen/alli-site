A space to be heard, understood, and supported.

The teen years can bring incredible growth—but they can also feel overwhelming.

School, friendships, identity, family relationships, anxiety, self-esteem, and the pressure to keep up can leave teens feeling stressed, isolated, or unsure of where to turn.

Therapy offers a supportive space where teens can explore what they're experiencing, build confidence, develop coping strategies, and discover new ways of navigating life's challenges.

Every teen is different, which is why therapy is never one-size-fits-all. Together, we'll find an approach that feels comfortable, meaningful, and supportive.

Areas of support

What brings you here today?

Anxiety & Stress

Support for excessive worry, panic, perfectionism, social anxiety, school stress, and feeling overwhelmed.

Depression & Low Mood

When motivation is low, life feels heavy, or it becomes difficult to enjoy the things that once mattered.

Neurodiversity

Support for autistic teens, ADHD, PDA profiles, and other neurodivergent ways of experiencing the world through a strengths-based, affirming approach.

Emotional Regulation

Learning to understand and manage intense emotions while building resilience and self-awareness.

Identity & 2SLGBTQIA+ Affirming Care

A welcoming, affirming space to explore identity, relationships, belonging, and self-acceptance.

Support with peer relationships, social challenges, conflict, communication, and building healthy connections.

Trauma

A gentle, individualized approach that supports healing at a pace that feels safe.

How sessions work

Therapy that meets teens where they are.

Not every teen wants to sit across from a therapist and talk for an hour—and that's okay.

Some teens feel most comfortable talking. Others find it easier to express themselves through creativity, music, movement, mindfulness, or other experiential approaches.

Some sessions may involve mostly conversation. Others may include creative or music-centred experiences. Many naturally evolve over time as trust develops.

There is no expectation to play an instrument, sing, or be creative. These approaches are simply additional tools that may be used if they support your goals and feel comfortable for you.

Therapy is always shaped around the individual—not the method.

Teens I commonly support

I work with teens who…

  • feel anxious or overwhelmed
  • struggle with self-esteem or confidence
  • experience depression or low mood
  • are navigating identity or belonging
  • are autistic or ADHD
  • find friendships or social situations challenging
  • are grieving a loss
  • have experienced trauma
  • feel like traditional talk therapy hasn't been the right fit
