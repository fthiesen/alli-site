Support for the people who support others.

Parenting can be deeply rewarding, but it can also be challenging, exhausting, and overwhelming, especially when your child experiences the world differently or needs additional support.

Whether you are navigating a new diagnosis, supporting your child's emotional well-being, or simply trying to understand how to best show up for your child, having a space to process your own experiences can be incredibly valuable.

Therapy provides an opportunity to explore your emotions, build resilience, develop self-compassion, and reconnect with yourself while continuing to support your child.

Areas of support

What brings you here?

Supporting a Neurodivergent Child

Parenting a neurodivergent child can bring unique joys and challenges.

Therapy can provide space to process emotions, navigate uncertainty, understand your own responses, and explore ways to support your relationship with your child.

Adjusting to a Diagnosis or New Understanding

A diagnosis can bring many emotions—relief, grief, confusion, hope, worry, or uncertainty.

You don't have to process those experiences alone.

Managing Stress & Caregiver Burnout

Supporting a child with complex needs can be demanding.

Therapy can provide a space to acknowledge your own needs, build coping strategies, and explore ways to care for yourself while caring for others.

Understanding Your Own Emotions

Sometimes parenting challenges bring up feelings that are difficult to understand—frustration, guilt, worry, sadness, or feeling overwhelmed.

Therapy can help you explore these experiences with compassion.

Strengthening Connection

Parenting is built on relationships.

Therapy can support reflection, communication, and a deeper understanding of yourself and your child.

My approach

Therapy that makes space for your experience

Parents and caregivers often spend so much time focusing on their child's needs that their own emotions and experiences get pushed aside.

Therapy provides a space where you can be supported as a person, not only as a parent or caregiver.

Sessions are primarily talk-based and may draw from approaches that support emotional regulation, self-understanding, coping, and resilience. When helpful and meaningful, creative approaches may also be incorporated.

For parents of neurodivergent children

As someone who works extensively with neurodivergent children and youth, I understand the unique experiences that families may encounter when navigating differences in communication, sensory needs, emotional regulation, and support systems.

My goal is to provide a compassionate space where parents and caregivers feel understood, supported, and less alone.

Complimentary consultation

You deserve support too.

Caring for others can be deeply meaningful, but you don't have to carry everything alone.

If you're looking for a space to process your experiences, better understand yourself, or receive support while navigating your role as a parent or caregiver, I welcome you to reach out.
