Therapy designed around you.

There is no single way to experience emotions, process challenges, or create change.

Some people find healing through conversation and reflection. Others need a more experiential approach that includes creativity, music, mindfulness, or other ways of exploring their thoughts and feelings.

My role is not to fit you into a specific therapy model. My role is to understand you, your experiences, and your goals—and work collaboratively to find an approach that feels meaningful and effective.

Therapy is a partnership. Together, we will explore what is happening, build on your strengths, and develop tools that support the changes you want to make.

Evidence-informed psychotherapy

Grounded in research, tailored to you.

My practice integrates evidence-informed therapeutic approaches based on your unique needs and goals.

Depending on what you are seeking support for, therapy may include exploring thoughts and patterns, developing emotional regulation skills, building self-compassion, strengthening coping strategies, increasing self-awareness, and creating meaningful change.

I draw from a range of therapeutic approaches, including cognitive and behavioural strategies, emotion regulation approaches, parts work, mindfulness-based practices, and other relational and experiential methods.

Rather than using a one-size-fits-all approach, I adapt therapy to what is most helpful for each individual.

More than words

Creative & expressive approaches

Words are powerful—but they are not the only way people communicate and process their experiences.

As a Music Psychotherapist with additional training in Expressive Arts Therapy, I recognize that creativity can offer another pathway for exploration, expression, and healing.

Music and creative approaches may support individuals who:

  • find emotions difficult to describe
  • process experiences in non-linear ways
  • feel more comfortable expressing themselves creatively
  • benefit from sensory or experiential approaches

These approaches are always optional. You do not need musical experience, artistic ability, or any previous creative background. For some clients, music may be central to sessions. For others, it may not be part of therapy at all.

The approach is guided by you.

Neurodiversity-affirming care

Honouring different ways of thinking, feeling, and being.

I believe that differences in communication, learning, attention, sensory experiences, and emotional regulation are important parts of who someone is—not things to be "fixed."

My approach to working with neurodivergent individuals is grounded in curiosity, respect, and acceptance.

I support children, teens, and adults with diverse needs, including individuals with autism, ADHD, PDA profiles, and other complex support needs.

Therapy is adapted to the individual, including their communication style, strengths, preferences, and goals.

A relationship-based approach

The therapeutic relationship matters.

Research consistently highlights the importance of the therapeutic relationship in creating meaningful change.

I strive to create a space where clients feel safe, respected, understood, and accepted.

Whether we are exploring difficult emotions, developing new skills, processing experiences, or simply beginning to understand yourself differently, therapy begins with trust.

2SLGBTQIA+ affirming care

A space where you can be yourself.

I am committed to providing affirming, inclusive care for members of the 2SLGBTQIA+ community.

Your identity, relationships, experiences, and story are welcomed and respected.

What therapy may look like

Therapy can look different for everyone.

Depending on your goals and preferences, sessions may involve a mix of the approaches here.

There is no expectation to participate in any specific activity. The most important part of therapy is finding an approach that feels authentic and useful for you.

  • Conversation and reflection
  • Learning practical coping strategies
  • Exploring emotions and patterns
  • Building self-awareness and self-compassion
  • Mindfulness practices
  • Creative expression
  • Music-based approaches
