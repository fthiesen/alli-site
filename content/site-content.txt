About page

Therapy should fit the person—not the other way around.
Seeking support can feel vulnerable. Whether you're reaching out for yourself, your child, or your family, finding a therapist who truly understands your needs can make all the difference.
My goal is to create a space where people feel safe, respected, and free to show up exactly as they are. Every person experiences the world differently, and I believe therapy should reflect that.
There is no single "right" way to heal. Together, we'll discover an approach that feels meaningful and works for you.
(Large warm photo of me in the studio/office.)
Meet Alli
I've always been drawn to the ways people express themselves beyond words.
Over the years, my work has shown me that healing doesn't always happen through conversation alone. Sometimes people need space to create, move, reflect, laugh, sit quietly, or simply be understood.
As a Registered Psychotherapist with specialized training in Music Psychotherapy and additional training in Expressive Arts Therapy, I bring a flexible, client-centred approach to every session. Some clients prefer traditional talk therapy, while others benefit from incorporating music, creative expression, mindfulness, or experiential approaches.
There is no expectation that you have any musical or artistic experience. These are simply tools that may be available if they support your goals.
Each session is shaped around what feels most helpful for you.
What working together looks like
Some sessions look very much like traditional psychotherapy and are entirely conversation-based.
Some include music, art, mindfulness, movement, or other creative experiences.
Many combine several approaches over time.
The focus is never on making music or creating art—it is on helping you move toward your goals in a way that feels natural, comfortable, and meaningful.
Therapy is adapted to you, not the other way around.

My Approach page

Therapy designed around you.
There is no single way to experience emotions, process challenges, or create change.
Some people find healing through conversation and reflection. Others need a more experiential approach that includes creativity, music, mindfulness, or other ways of exploring their thoughts and feelings.
My role is not to fit you into a specific therapy model. My role is to understand you, your experiences, and your goals—and work collaboratively to find an approach that feels meaningful and effective.
Therapy is a partnership. Together, we will explore what is happening, build on your strengths, and develop tools that support the changes you want to make.
Evidence-Informed Psychotherapy
My practice integrates evidence-informed therapeutic approaches based on your unique needs and goals.
Depending on what you are seeking support for, therapy may include exploring thoughts and patterns, developing emotional regulation skills, building self-compassion, strengthening coping strategies, increasing self-awareness, and creating meaningful change.
I draw from a range of therapeutic approaches, including cognitive and behavioural strategies, emotion regulation approaches, parts work, mindfulness-based practices, and other relational and experiential methods.
Rather than using a one-size-fits-all approach, I adapt therapy to what is most helpful for each individual.
Then...
More Than Words: Creative & Expressive Approaches
Words are powerful—but they are not the only way people communicate and process their experiences.
As a Music Psychotherapist with additional training in Expressive Arts Therapy, I recognize that creativity can offer another pathway for exploration, expression, and healing.
Music and creative approaches may support individuals who:
find emotions difficult to describe
process experiences in non-linear ways
feel more comfortable expressing themselves creatively
benefit from sensory or experiential approaches
These approaches are always optional. You do not need musical experience, artistic ability, or any previous creative background.
For some clients, music may be central to sessions. For others, it may not be part of therapy at all.
The approach is guided by you.

Fees & OAP page
Fees & Ontario Autism Program (OAP)
Individualized psychotherapy support that is accessible and responsive to your needs.
Finding the right therapist for your child or yourself is an important decision. I believe therapy should be both meaningful and accessible, which is why I aim to provide clear information about fees, funding options, and available supports.
As a Registered Psychotherapist with specialized training in Music Psychotherapy and Expressive Arts Therapy, I provide individualized psychotherapy that can incorporate a range of approaches depending on each person's needs, strengths, and goals.
Ontario Autism Program (OAP) Funding
Core Clinical Services – Psychotherapy
Families accessing the Ontario Autism Program may be eligible to use funding toward psychotherapy services under Core Clinical Services.
As a Registered Psychotherapist, my services may be eligible for OAP funding.
OAP funding eligibility and available amounts vary based on each child's approved funding plan. Families are encouraged to review their individual OAP information or speak with their OAP care coordinator to confirm eligibility.
If you are unsure whether my services may be a good fit for your child, I welcome you to reach out for a consultation.
A Unique Approach to OAP-Funded Psychotherapy
Many children and youth—particularly those who are neurodivergent—may not always benefit from traditional talk-based approaches alone.
As a Music Psychotherapist, I bring specialized training in using music and creative approaches within a psychotherapy framework. These approaches can offer additional pathways for communication, emotional expression, self-awareness, and connection.
Depending on the individual, therapy may include:
Conversation and reflection
Emotional regulation strategies
Creative expression
Music-based approaches
Experiential activities
Strength-based exploration
There is no expectation that a child or youth be musical or have any previous creative experience. These approaches are simply tools that may be used when they are meaningful and supportive.
For some clients, music may be a central part of therapy. For others, sessions may look more like traditional psychotherapy.
The approach is always guided by the individual.
